# Intake — human-inference

Business context mapped from founder notes and reviewed by Muxin at kickoff. Original notes, model inferences, supporting quotes, and corrections are preserved in intake-context.json. These answers are reviewed interpretations, not verbatim quotations unless identified there.

## Block A: what are we building?

**What are you helping people do?**

 Human Inference helps people build the future they want to live in:
 overwhelm → clarity and agency, despair → hope, outrage → joyful creation.

**Who will you help first?**

Voters in America

**What problem do they already know they have?**

They know the system is broken but don't know what to do about it


**What do you think is broken for them?**

The system creates manufactured complexity and leaves ordinary people without clear, practical levers for action.

**If this works, what gets better for them?**

If this works, readers move from overwhelm, despair, or outrage toward clarity, agency, hope, and joyful creation, including taking a meaningful concrete action.

## Block B: what makes you credible?

**What have you built, shared, taught, or tested?**

You have built and tested Voter Choice, created a private local dictation tool you use daily, and developed substantial writing and software projects.

**What proof shows that people care?**

People have expressed excitement and support for the vision of Voter Choice, but there is not yet evidence of product use, paid demand, or sustained behavior.

**What have people thanked you for?**

People have thanked you for writing about how not to burn out and how to sustain joyful social work. People have also responded positively to the Voter Choice vision, but no product use or paid demand is established.

**What do you know from experience that others often miss?**

You combine anthropology, business, product, and lived experience with ADHD to spot assumptions and turn around difficult programs.

**What proof should we keep using?**

Continue using concrete personal experience, demonstrations, historical examples, and practical tools as proof-bearing material. Audience response remains the evidence to preserve and reuse.

## Block C: what does the audience feel?

**What frustrates them now?**

They feel overwhelmed by how much is broken and unsure how to act.

**What do they waste time trying to learn?**

They waste time trying to understand complex systems, politics, candidates, and what actions might actually help.

**What do they not trust?**

They do not trust shareholder-driven claims of social change, opaque systems, or complexity without practical action.

**What do they want to do but keep putting off?**

They want to make a meaningful difference but keep postponing action because the problems feel too large or unsolvable.

**What words do they use when they talk about this problem?**

Their language includes “everything feels broken,” “what can I even do,” and feeling “alone or crazy for trying.”

## Block D: what can the creator sustain?

**What format feels easiest for you: writing, video, audio, live teaching, demos, or templates?**

Writing, including short and longer written posts, feels most natural. Video is easier when it is an interview or live conversation. Images are possible but less easy.

**What can you make in under an hour and still enjoy?**

A short written post drawn from existing material.

**Which platform feels most natural?**

Substack is the current primary home for the writing, but whether it is the best place to grow remains unknown.

**Which platform would tire you out fastest?**

Monologue-style video, especially staring at a screen and talking to a camera.

**How much time can you give this for the next 14 days?**

A few hours a week feels definitely possible, with up to eight hours available if needed, alongside software work.

## Block E: what might the first offer become?

**What should the first paid offer be: a guide, toolkit, short course, community, coaching, or software-supported product? (a hypothesis -- must not preselect the final product format before audience evidence arrives)**

The current monetization hypothesis is a free-core, premium-subscription version of Voter Choice. Other possibilities remain open, including paid writing, books, and productized tools.

**What small win can someone get in 10 minutes?**

A person can take one small, concrete civic or creative action, such as checking one representative, making one introduction, or completing one short exercise.

**What bigger win can someone get in one or two weeks?**

A meaningful one- or two-week win is a reader moving through the funnel and, where relevant, taking one concrete civic or creative action. The specific action may include using Voter Choice, while the exact offer remains open.

**What must this business never become?**

The business should not become primarily client-management or consulting work. It should also focus on citizen engagement rather than only supporting institutions.

**What would make the first 14 days worth continuing?**

The first 14 days should show movement in subscribers, growth, leads, or sales while also producing useful reader responses and an enjoyable writing rhythm.
