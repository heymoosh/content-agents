In 1971 a corporate lawyer wrote a strategic memo to preserve the system of free enterprise that creates American prosperity: Powell’s memo focused heavily on changing the anti-business narratives of the time through sponsoring scholars, speakers, advertisers, and media that support pro-business views.

He emphasizes the need for companies to organize and wield political power, especially in court.

The real turns in his strategy are the moral ones - that free enterprise equals freedom itself, compared in stark contrast to totalitarian states. This was during the Red Scare of the Cold War so keep in mind that anything that smelled like communism was treated like a threat to American social stability.

But here’s what he gets wrong - a pro-business agenda does not equal a pro-society agenda. Society is a complex system, and if we only focus on improving one area it inevitably causes issues for the whole. It’s like having your doctor only monitor your blood pressure to see if you’re healthy.

The risk we have right now with AI and an understandable rise in populism is forgetting that other parts of society are also important and that each part serves each other. We have to sustain a healthy system across the board.

But right now, we’re showing symptoms of hyper capitalism: too much wealth is concentrated at the top but can’t even be spent, legislative capture has turned a productive free market of innovation into rent seeking and extraction through rules rigging, social mobility has declined with each generation and the social contract of working hard to attain wealth is threatened by automation.

So we very much need our own strategic memo to correct the situation - but without the blinders that pretend only one part of society matters more than the rest.
