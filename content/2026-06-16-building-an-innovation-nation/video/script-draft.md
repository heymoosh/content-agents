What if the smartest AI still can't tell you if your product idea is any good?

That's the gap I keep running into. AI can execute almost anything you scope for it. But taste, judgment, knowing what good looks like: that's still ours. And if that gap holds, the question isn't how fast we build better models. It's whether we build a nation of people who know how to use them.

Here's the problem. We raise kids to memorize answers, not ask questions. We reward certainty over curiosity. Then we send them into companies chasing quarterly numbers, where talking to customers is a checkbox, not a practice. We've made ourselves mechanical. Which is exactly the kind of work AI is best at replacing.

Meanwhile the traits that actually drive innovation, determination, resilience, judgment, don't belong to a privileged few. They can come from anywhere. But we've built a country that only recognizes them when they show up wrapped in the right school or the right zip code.

For a long time, investing broadly in people got dismissed as naive. Nice idea, can't afford it. I don't think that's true anymore. If judgment is the scarce resource, and judgment only grows when people have time and safety to develop it, then betting on people isn't charity. It's strategy.

Give people a real shot to become someone worth betting on. That might be the most economically valuable thing we can do.
