I used to think there were two ways to respond to a broken world: Ignore it, or let it consume me.

I assumed that trying to make a difference would only make the disappointment worse. There is already so much wrong. What if I tried and failed?

It turned out I was making the pain worse by doing nothing.

The professional content I was creating felt like I was holding my breath. Networking events left me empty. I felt like I was living inside a bubble with my head stuck in the sand while the world became more chaotic.

Then I gave myself three months to treat the work I actually cared about as an experiment.

I didn’t suddenly solve anything. I had conversations, built a voting tool, and wrote down what I was learning.

One day I remembered myself at eleven years old, imagining that I might help society in some way. I knew that if she could see me now, she would be proud.

If you have fifteen minutes today, open whatever notes app you already use and finish this sentence:

“If my eleven-year-old self could watch what I did for the next fifteen minutes, she would be proud to see me…”

Set a timer and do whatever you wrote.

If nothing comes to mind, borrow something I did: Send a useful tool to one person, schedule one conversation about something you care about, or check how one representative voted.
