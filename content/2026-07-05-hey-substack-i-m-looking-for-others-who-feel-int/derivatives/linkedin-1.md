I'm looking for others who feel intellectually lonely in tech.

I often see tech dominated by a certain kind of psyche: engineering optimized, startup growth focused, technocratic assumptions that tech alone solves everything.

If you're not hitting $XYZ M ARR, growing 50% YoY, getting Sequoia or a16z or Y Combinator level investors, you're invisible.

Tech talks big about wanting to change the world but focuses more on getting a lucrative exit, being bought out, and serving shareholders.

Their grand ideas for society are to serve B2B customers or become a digital consciousness and interplanetary species.

I love what software and AI can do to solve real problems: make policy, academia, and legalese easy to digest, take away the boring but necessary admin work that runs our lives, conduct civilian research into social problems, build tools to help us be more engaged citizens.

But these things don't make investors happy, so they don't get attention.

I get it: enterprise business mostly makes more profit sustainably and is less of a gamble than consumer products. But one of the things I've been looking forward to with AI is the opportunity to tackle niche markets and problems that weren't profitable to attempt before.

Now, with a $200 a month Claude MAX subscription, you can build the software, create and distribute the content, and keep on top of daily life while you're doing it.

There ought to be more people trying to solve real problems for everyone, not just for the already rich and wealthy.
