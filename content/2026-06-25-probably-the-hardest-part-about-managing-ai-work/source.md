Probably the hardest part about managing AI workflows is choosing what to focus on. I've had up to 14 projects spinning up all at once - and none of them have been fully realized. 

Building something goes beyond just the codebase - it’s also diving into the problem domain, thinking through the solution more deeply, creating  community around it. 

People have been mentioning how the challenge now is ‘distribution’ now that the building part is easier. I’d take it a step further - the business moats have now shifted from technical to human ones: Developing a brand, fostering loyalty authentically, creating stories that resonate - so that people ‘distribute’ the product for you. Word of mouth has always been the dominant way to grow software, and that won’t change IMO.

All of the things that still require your own perspective and human judgement are now even more important than ever. They likely will be the only thing that remains as a differentiator as AI takes on more and more knowledge work. 

What AI enables us to do is take on MORE of what used to take other humans to handle because the scope of running a business on your own was too big to handle. When I look at the 3 remaining projects I still invest in, I realize I still had to pick just 1 so that I could nurture it properly.

It’s really, really painful. I have strong faith and believe in all three of them. 

But there’s one that’s timely for this year, especially on the United States’ 250th anniversary and when a huge portion of Congress is up for reelection in November. At the same time, prominent AI leaders like Dario Amodei are forecasting job displacement and the arrival of AI models strong enough to take on nearly all knowledge work in as little as 1-5 years (and that was written half a year ago). 

So I’m going to do what little I can to push towards a future I believe in: I’ve been building a tool to help people evaluate their Congressmen based on donations and voting history. It evaluates actual behavior - not platform promises - and even scores it based on how well aligned your Congressmen’s behaviors are to your preferred stances on issues you care about.

We elected representatives to do the job of governing and policy making for us so that we can focus on our own jobs and lives. We pay their salaries through our taxes. 

Yet Congress spends up to 70% of their time just fundraising for their next election instead of doing their jobs. Fundraising is the #1 predictor of a successful campaign - not whether they’ve done their actual jobs.

I think it’s time we fix that broken incentive system.
