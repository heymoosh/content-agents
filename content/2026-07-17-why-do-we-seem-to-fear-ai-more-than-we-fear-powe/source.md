"Why do we seem to fear AI more than we fear power?"

Yeah, definitely - that's a gap I see all the time on X and tech discourse. People worrying about permanent underclasses and that limiting frontier AI models will create economic inequality when - hello, being poor in America is already a permanent underclass, and when was the last time you looked at the US Gini wealth index?

You're right to call out the dangers of emergency power consolidation. I wonder if there's been any lessons in history to draw from - where a society was able to effectively respond to a real threat without needing to grant supernormal powers.

It seems to me that AI researchers are genuinely worried about a rogue AI, which makes the situation complicated. It's an unprecedented event and history may not tell us everything we need to know. Dario also points out the other scenarios of bad actors/nations using AI in Adolescence of Technology, but the challenge of power seems as old as civilization, like you mentioned before.

But hey, maybe this time we'll figure it out. I often give social problems to Claude to see whether our unchallenged assumptions about society, wealth, or power are proven by what's in the data. And now they have Claude Science with even better data viz and datasets!

Maybe we can even model social power structures with different agents and incentives to see what happens, or have AI 'figure out how to keep power in check' in a simulated social environment and see what it comes up with. Apparently Anthropic uses that approach to auto-research questions:

https://www.anthropic.com/institute/recursive-self-improvement

It feels like the hardest thing in all this is the same thing that founders and scientists on the cutting edge face daily - believing it's possible, showing up daily to keep going, even if there's no guarantee of success at the end. But often times that persistence is the very thing that separates the successful founders from the rest, according to Y Combinator.
