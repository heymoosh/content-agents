When the problems are enormous, it’s easy to conclude that nothing we do matters.

But institutions depend on ordinary people for more than we usually acknowledge. They need our votes, labor, money, attention, trust, and our willingness to care for one another when their systems leave people behind.

None of those things fixes everything. They’re simply places where we are not powerless.

When I wanted to do something about money in politics, I built Voter Choice so people could look at how their representatives actually voted without doing hours of research.

When I started talking to people working on democracy, I found people with projects that needed skills someone else had. I began connecting them instead of trying to get everyone to agree on one big project.

Even buying groceries can be a choice. I buy expensive pasture-raised eggs because I want to support independent farmers, regenerative agriculture, and happy hens. Have you seen how cute they are?

If you want to do something right now, use [Voter Choice](https://voterchoice.vote/) to check one representative on one issue. Or introduce two people who should know each other. Or redirect one purchase you were already going to make.

Choose whatever is easiest.

I wrote a fuller map of these options in [The world’s broken. What do we do?](https://www.humaninference.ai/essays/the-worlds-broken-what-do-we-do)
