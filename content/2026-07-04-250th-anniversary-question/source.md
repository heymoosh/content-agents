*Note: Not sure I wanted to write this right now. I don't want to dampen the celebrations - they look awesome. Plus people are on holiday. But - this has been juggling around in my head, so here goes nothing.*

>Four score and seven years ago our fathers brought forth on this continent, a new nation, conceived in Liberty, and dedicated to the proposition that all men are created equal.
>
>Now we are engaged in a great civil war, testing whether that nation, or any nation so conceived and so dedicated, can long endure.
>
>- *Republican at Gettysburg, 1863*

As America celebrates its 250th anniversary amid [political tensions](https://www.npr.org/2026/06/30/nx-s1-5839358/birthright-citizenship-decision-scotus-trump) and [data center protests](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/data-center-opposition-sharply-rising-2026-study-finds-rcna349728), one has to wonder if we'll make it to the 300th year.

AI promises to deliver many [incredible miracles](https://a16z.com/the-techno-optimist-manifesto/), but all of them assume we'll still have a society capable of working together towards shared interests. Yet the [booing of pro-AI commencement speakers](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/former-google-ceo-booed-graduation-speech-ai-rcna345585) and the [violence targeting Sam Altman's home](https://www.npr.org/2026/04/13/g-s1-117320/openai-sam-altman-molotov-cocktail) this year suggest we're anything but.

Technologists and Silicon Valley pundits wonder if the reaction is [simple Ludditism](https://www.npr.org/2026/06/19/nx-s1-5853589/luddite-meaning-history-ai) rejecting any new technology, or from feeling threatened by AI's superior capabilities. But to me it's very clear where the anger is coming from: People aren't rejecting AI's promises. They're rejecting a society in which those promises will be denied to so many, yet achieved at their expense through the extraction or exploitation of their data, labor, mental health and civic rights.

### We, the People

We, the People of the United States, are tired of being told to just work harder.

We're tired of claims that our society is fair and rewards meritocracy and hard work when:
- [Banks were bailed out](https://www.propublica.org/article/the-bailout-was-11-years-ago-were-still-tracking-every-penny) of the financial crisis with taxpayer's money, while regular people lost their homes due to the irresponsibility of those exact banks making money off of subprime mortgages given to people who had no chances of paying it back
- [Executives get paid lavish bonuses after they lay off a workforce](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jackkelly/2020/06/10/ceos-are-making-millions-while-laying-off-thousands-of-workers-and-filing-for-bankruptcy/) due to a strategy that they themselves were responsible for making poor decisions on
- Rich families are essentially [buying their children's entrance into elite colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varsity_Blues_scandal) - which had promised to be a bastion of meritocratic success and a fair path towards greater prosperity for many
- Businesses were kept running during COVID by exposing front-line workers to a virus that did not have a cure, while paying them so little that [McDonald's literally advises their workers to use taxpayer subsidized food stamps](https://abcnews.go.com/Business/mcdonalds-employee-helpline-shows-workers-food-stamps/story?id=20668024) to get by
- [College tuition loans with outrageous terms](https://protectborrowers.org/shadow-student-debt-study/) are marketed to young adults like loan sharks, crippling their ability to earn wealth
- Entire towns lost stability and income when [factory jobs were lost to automation and labor outsourcing](https://www.industryweek.com/talent/article/22028380/the-abandonment-of-small-cities-in-the-rust-belt) to cheaper countries, because of fiduciary responsibility to increase shareholder profits by cutting costs
- Families having to decide between rent and groceries as costs keep climbing, while [corporations crow about record profits and cutting down their workforce with AI](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/jobless-boom-ai-economy-labor-market-corporate-profits-layoffs/)
- CEOs are paid [nearly 300x the average salary of their workers](https://www.epi.org/publication/ceo-pay-in-2024/) while studies have shown [nearly zero proof of high executive compensation leading to long-term business performance](https://www.msci.com/www/research-paper/are-ceos-paid-for-performance-/0412607620)
- Politicians claim democracy is a sacred right while passing legislation that allows for [money to buy representation outright through professional lobbyists, corporations, PACs, dark money](https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/research-reports/citizens-united-explained), and to [sponsor media and academic institutions dedicated to serving their political narratives](https://www.desmog.com/2011/08/23/40th-anniversary-lewis-powell-memo-launching-corporate-propaganda-infrastructure/), creating a system in which the wealthiest in society are allowed to purchase and use megaphones to shout their directives while leaving everyone else effectively silenced
- [Black applicants are denied home mortgages at 2x the rate of white applicants](https://www.minneapolisfed.org/article/2024/lender-reported-reasons-for-mortgage-denials-dont-explain-racial-disparities), despite equal financial profiles

We're tired of being told this is the only system that works and that it's necessary for broader prosperity.

We want a society that honors and protects the right to life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness for everyone, not just for the few and already privileged.

### Let Them Eat Cake

Notified of French peasants revolting out of hunger, Marie Antoinette famously said, "Let them eat cake."

Regardless of historic accuracy, it's an effective image of elite disconnection from the realities of common people. The irony today is hearing that same melody through a different tune of technocratic utopian promises: AI will [end wage labor and create abundance for everyone](https://blog.samaltman.com/abundant-intelligence), advance all scientific and technical fields, creating massive economic growth and wealth while solving climate change and [curing cancer](https://ia.samaltman.com/), taking us to our next step in human evolution as we [transcend our biological forms and become uploaded digital consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Singularity_Is_Near) exploring the universe as an [interstellar species](https://x.com/elonmusk/status/1838661749349781736).

It doesn't bode well that the elites of today have too much in common with the elites in Antoinette's time, nor is it reassuring that our Gini index measure of wealth inequality likely matches that of the period predating the bloody French Revolution.*

*Footnote - that is estimated based on what we know. We have a decent idea of income Gini for that period in France (0.59), this claim is using an AI estimated wealth Gini (0.75 - 0.85) compared to the US's wealth Gini (0.83). Why not use income Gini for apples to apples comparison? Because in a capitalist society, the growth of wealth through assets greatly exceeds growth via income. Total wealth is a fairer measurement of total inequality, IMO.

### What Now
*I made a pledge that I would never write an article about society and leave readers with a sense of despair. I will always provide something that you can do about it. This is that section.*

We didn't get here by accident - a mix of [self-serving incentives](https://www.greenpeace.org/usa/democracy/the-lewis-powell-memo-a-corporate-blueprint-to-dominate-democracy/), fear, obsolete knowledge, and honest belief this was a good idea were likely part of the decisions that created the systems and institutions we have today. I plan on covering that in more detail in later articles.

But we don't have to continue something that no longer makes sense for the world we live in. We can forgive the past for making mistakes, being unable to predict the future or to know everything, and focus on building better from what we know today.

I have a few suggestions for navigating the messy 'figuring out phase' as you make your own decisions about how to approach these challenges:

**Don't lump all wealthy people into the same category and treat them like enemies.** 

Despite this article's tone, I don't believe all wealthy and powerful people are against us. Notable examples include [Nick Hanauer](https://www.nickhanauer.com/nick-on-economics) (early Amazon investor), who has been [challenging the laws and neoliberal economics](https://www.ted.com/talks/nick_hanauer_beware_fellow_plutocrats_the_pitchforks_are_coming) that contribute to wealth inequality in America. [Dario Amodei](https://darioamodei.com), whose essays cover broad concerns for AI's impact on society, and [whose company holds the line on AI safety and democratic values despite it causing friction for product launches and lucrative government deals](https://www.anthropic.com/news/statement-department-of-war).

I'm sure you'll find your own examples. The important thing is to find allies, and powerful wealthy ones are extremely useful allies. But they can't form a coalition with the rest of us if we refuse to give them the invitation.

**Extend curiosity, compassion, and community to the 'other side' of the political spectrum.**

Part of the reason why the current systems continue to persist is because most normal citizens stopped agreeing on things. This was by design - through the [capture of media and academia by powerful interests](https://scholarlycommons.law.wlu.edu/powellmemo/1/) to promote their worldviews, giving us their perspectives through those loud megaphones they own. 

It was also partly by accident - by social media algorithms optimizing for profit maximizing engagement due to public tech companies' fiduciary responsibility to shareholders (or even private tech companies' fiduciary responsibilities to their investors). It turns out, [rage baiting and showing content of dubious factual integrity](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aap9559) are all fair game to get engagement metrics to rise.

Whatever the case, part of the reason things aren't changing is because we can't even agree on what the facts are anymore, and it prevents us from being able to organize a unifying coalition to counter powerful actors. 

So I propose a different approach - not focusing on facts, but on community, values and relationships.

Most political conversations have an [interesting trait](https://perceptiongap.us/): People seem to agree on values and what they want for society. The point where we diverge depends on which source of information everyone is citing from.

Since we can't fix the latter (yet), let's build off of the former. Keep conversations focused on what we all care about, on the problems we want to see solved, and figuring out how we may solve for it. Be curious and listen without anger or judgement, and share without trying to convert.

We all lose by trying to 'win' the argument and be right - but we stand to be powerful when we embrace our humanness, values, and belief that we'll figure it out together.

**Vote in the midterms on November - but especially in your primary (if still in season).**

I'm not politically aware - seriously. I didn't know what a primary was until this year. But it's probably even more important than those big November elections you'll no doubt be seeing ads about for the remainder of the year.

The primary is when we get to vote who even runs in the race. [So few people show up](https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2025/06/26/voter-turnout-2020-2024/) that it takes very little to swing the outcomes. By the time the big November elections come around, some of the decision has already been decided by the primary.

If there is still a primary in your state, I highly encourage you to vote.

If there isn't, go vote in November - actually show up for a non-presidential election. This year is a big year - [the entire House of Congress and 1/3 of the Senate will be up for election](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-history-tells-us-about-the-2026-midterm-elections/).

Most people skip these - we don't tend to tune in unless it's The Big Presidential Election. But Congress matters a lot more than you think - they represent you at the state and federal level, and they have a huge sway in which legislation gets passed.

So it behooves all of us to go vote.
