## Profile

Ingested from Muxin's own pre-existing Obsidian vault research (originally gathered for a job search, now repurposed as client-lead evidence). Mem (Mem Labs) is an AI-native personal-knowledge app founded in 2019 by Stanford friends Kevin Moody (CEO) and Dennis Xu, growing out of a 2014 dorm-conversation vision of a "Me API," a personal knowledge graph the user, not a platform, owns. It raised roughly $29M total, including a $23.5M Series A co-led by OpenAI's Startup Fund (November 2022, roughly $110M post-money). After a rough 2023-2024 stretch, reworked and removed features, public user complaints, and Dennis Xu's clean departure in January 2024 to found Adaptive Computer, a roughly 12-person team spent 20 months rebuilding the product from scratch and relaunched in 2025 as "Mem 2.0," repositioning from a notes/PKM tool to an "AI Thought Partner": automatic voice and meeting capture, AI tagging and related-notes surfacing, Mem Chat (retrieval over your own notes), and Heads Up (proactive pre-meeting resurfacing). It's a paid-only product (roughly $15/mo, no free tier) aimed at solo professionals, competing in a category increasingly squeezed by ChatGPT's and Claude's own expanding memory and agent features, a real, still-unresolved existential-moat question the vault's own research surfaces repeatedly, independent of any Mem messaging.

## Classification

Turnaround. E1 (vault-corroborated 2023-24 feature removal and user churn), E2 (Kevin's own confirmation of a 12-person, 20-month ground-up rebuild), and E3 (explicit public repositioning, "we're no longer just a notes app") together satisfy the rubric's "visibly different core product versions over time" and "public pivot language" checklist items with genuine primary-source and vault-corroborated evidence, this is the vault's own independent research (Kevin's LinkedIn, Mem's blog, and a separate synthesis of press/video commentary) arriving at the same conclusion from different sources, not a restatement of the JSA snapshot's "2.0 rebuild" note. The HARD qualifier (open to changing their mind) is met at the highest-cost level available, a full product rebuild, not just messaging, and E5 shows the same instinct as an explicitly articulated belief, not just an action. E4 is secondary corroboration, consistent with a real strategic reset rather than a value-destroying blowup.

Disconfirmation pass: checked for evidence against this read. Found none of the rubric's disqualifying markers, no "just execute" job-description language (Mem has zero open roles right now, so there's no posting language to check), and no evidence of a long-tenured exec layer locking the roadmap. The one real caveat is E6: Kevin is the sole product decision-maker, so "openness to changing minds" may in practice be bounded to one person's judgment rather than distributed through the org. Checked the greenfield checklist directly and found no supporting evidence: no recent funding (last raise was November 2022, roughly 3.5 years stale), no founding-PM/0-to-1 job language (no postings exist), no founder content describing active, ongoing exploration of direction beyond the already-completed 2.0 rebuild. Also worth flagging as unresolved background risk, not a rubric signal, but real: the vault's own research independently surfaces a genuine, still-open existential-competition question, whether Mem's "thought partner" wedge survives Claude/ChatGPT's expanding memory features, which doesn't change the classification but is relevant to how confidently Muxin should frame the pitch.
