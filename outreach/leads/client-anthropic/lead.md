## Profile

Ingested from Muxin's own pre-existing Obsidian vault research (originally gathered for a job search, now repurposed as client-lead evidence). Anthropic is a San Francisco-based frontier AI lab (Claude models, Claude Code, Claude in Chrome, Cowork, MCP, Skills, and an enterprise/API business) founded by Dario and Daniela Amodei, positioning itself simultaneously as a commercial AI company and a safety/public-benefit-focused one ("I think of us less as an AI safety company, and more a company that's focused on public benefit," Dario Amodei). It is scaling fast on both the model side and the product side: alongside its core Claude product org, it runs an internal incubator called Anthropic Labs (co-led by former Chief Product Officer Mike Krieger and Ben Mann), which pairs a conviction-driven builder with an engineer per bet, runs roughly two-week evaluation cycles, and explicitly expects most bets to be killed. Publicly, its product and leadership voice repeatedly narrates its own build mistakes on the record (an over-personalized Artifact launch, a too-early enterprise-wide Claude AI redesign rollout, a Claude Code source-leak "process failure," reversing a stated "no onboarding flow" principle) rather than defending them, and its hiring language explicitly screens for "low ego, high debate" and candidates who can name times they changed their mind because of research or safety feedback.

## Classification

Per E1, E2, and E9, Anthropic Labs (Krieger's post-CPO incubator) is a live, currently-hiring, single-PM 0-to-1 unit whose own hiring language mirrors the rubric's "founding PM"/0-to-1 category directly, not adjacent-sounding, a greenfield read. The HARD qualifier (open to changing minds) is well supported, not assumed: E3, E4, E7, E9, and E10 are five separate, current, named-individual instances of Anthropic people or its own hiring process explicitly rewarding or enacting a changed mind, spanning the CEO's safety instincts (E3, about Boris Cherny), core product-org iteration (E4, E7), a named PM's product-principle reversal (E10), and the hiring bar itself (E9). No turnaround signal was found anywhere across the eighteen vault files reviewed (no reposted roles, tenure gaps, pivot language, or Glassdoor/Blind turnaround commentary), so this is not a turnaround read.

Disconfirmation pass: specifically searched for turnaround signals (reposted/reopened roles, leadership tenure gaps, pivot language, Glassdoor/Blind commentary about repeated direction changes) across all eighteen files and found none, the closest thing was Krieger's own reorg from CPO into Labs, which reads as an expansion move, not distress. Also specifically searched for disqualifying language ("we know what we're building, just need execution," locked-roadmap framing, heavy internal politics) and found none matching that bar; the only lock-in-flavored line found anywhere is E8, and it concerns Claude's model character/constitution, not product or org direction, so it does not clear the rubric's disqualifying threshold. One caution surfaced only in Muxin's own private analysis, paraphrased and not quoted: she flags a forward-looking risk that Labs' current low-bureaucracy, single-PM culture could calcify into more politics as it scales, a risk to watch, not present-state evidence against the qualifier.
