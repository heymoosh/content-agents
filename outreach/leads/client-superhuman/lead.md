## Profile

Ingested from Muxin's own pre-existing Obsidian vault research (originally gathered for a job search, now repurposed as client-lead evidence). Superhuman is the current brand name of what began as Grammarly Inc. After acquiring Coda (December 2024) and the original, independent Superhuman email app (July 2025), Grammarly renamed its parent corporate entity to "Superhuman" in October 2025 and folded everything into a four-product "AI-native productivity suite": Grammarly (writing assistant, the original and still-largest revenue line), Coda (workspace/docs), Superhuman Mail (the email client founded by Rahul Vohra in 2014, formerly the independent "Superhuman"), and Superhuman Go (a new proactive AI-agent platform launched alongside the rebrand). Scale: roughly 40M daily active users, $700M+ ARR, roughly 1,500 employees. The combined company is run by CEO Shishir Mehrotra (ex-YouTube CPO/CTO, Coda co-founder, became Grammarly CEO January 2025), not by Rahul Vohra, who now leads Mail as a product division reporting up to Mehrotra. Decision-making authority sits with Mehrotra's platform/process-driven leadership, not Vohra's craft-driven original-Superhuman culture.

## Classification

Disqualified. The HARD qualifier (open to changing their mind) fails at the level that actually controls decisions: E4 and E5 show CEO Shishir Mehrotra, under sustained, repeated direct challenge on the exact kind of question Muxin's approach would raise (who is harmed, not just whether the legal bar was cleared), holding the same "attribution" framing without ever conceding the substantive point, even while agreeing the feature itself was bad and killing it. That is a company killing a symptom while the decision-maker's underlying framing stayed fixed. E1 and E2 show real turnaround-shaped upheaval (ethics blowup, messy three-way merger integration), which might otherwise read as fertile ground for Muxin's untested-assumption diagnostic, but E9 (Muxin's own prior, independently reached verdict) already concluded the mothership's culture, not Rahul's, is what would actually govern any engagement.

Disconfirmation pass: the counter-evidence is real. E8's "that was a mistake, we're going to fix it" shows a leadership-team-level habit of updating after failure, and E6 shows Superhuman Go is a genuine live experiment, not a locked roadmap. But E8 is Rahul characterizing the merged leadership generally, not Shishir specifically on the one instance where his openness was actually tested (E4/E5), and on that instance he did not update. Nothing found flips this: no quote from Shishir conceding the harm framing, and no evidence the org chart changed after the incident. The disqualification stands at the company level.
