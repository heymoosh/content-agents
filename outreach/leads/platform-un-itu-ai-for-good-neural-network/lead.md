## Profile

AI for Good Neural Network is the year-round digital community and content platform run by the UN's International Telecommunication Union (ITU), in partnership with 40+ UN agencies and the Government of Switzerland. It hosts a "Discovery Series" of webinars, virtual exhibitions, and the flagship annual AI for Good Global Summit in Geneva (most recently 7-10 July 2026), organized around the UN Sustainable Development Goals. It claims 55,000+ members and 37,000+ contributors across 180+ countries. Unlike a single show or newsletter, content, speaker slots, and community access here are all mediated by a formal ITU review process rather than an individual host or editor.

## Classification

Topic overlap is real (E1), and recency is excellent, the platform is mid-event this same week (E6). But the other three dimensions are thin or contradictory. Audience is institutional-scale and well past the mid-tail cap with weak individual-engagement evidence (E2, E3). The worldview-match quote (E7) is only an adjacent echo, about access and benefit, not the "no say in how it's built" thrust of the worldview-map statement it's closest to, so the values leg is not cleanly met. Guest-friendliness has a real formal channel (E4) but it is competitively gated, currently closed for this cycle, and there is a documented, on-point account (E5, E8) of ITU editorially suppressing critical content naming specific companies, exactly the kind of business-practice critique that is core to Muxin's typical pitch angle. With the values leg unmet and guest-friendliness real-but-constrained-and-contradicted, this does not clear the bar for `partial`; per the rubric, thin/mixed evidence should not be rounded up.

**Sharper framing (Muxin's PR #177 review comment, 2026-07-10):** Muxin, who has looked at this network herself, describes it as "sort of like SMA, a directory of 'people interested in caring about stuff,' but it's not necessarily a platform unless they had some series in which they interviewed builders like me." That's exactly what E2-E4 already show without naming it as such: 55k+ "members" and 37k+ "contributors" (E2) is directory-scale membership, not an audience tuned to a recurring show; the one real hosting mechanism, the "Become a speaker" application (E4), is a competitive, editorially-gated summit CFP, not a standing interview series with an open door. Under the platform fit profile's 3-part test (`config/outreach/platforms.md`), a directory or network is not automatically a "platform" just because it is large and topically adjacent: it only clears the guest-friendliness leg if it has an actual interview/guest/conversation mechanism genuinely open to outside people, and here that mechanism is a closed CFP cycle layered with a documented incident (E5, E8) of the org editorially constraining a critical guest's content. That reframes why this stays `weak` rather than `partial`: it isn't that guest-friendliness is merely "unconfirmed," it's that the one channel found behaves like a directory's gated call for submissions, not an open invitation to talk, which is the harder failure mode of dimension 4. Verdict unchanged: `weak`.

Disconfirmation pass: Searched for evidence against both the worldview-match and the guest-friendliness read. Found a documented 2025 incident (Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists, E5/E8) where ITU pressured a critical keynote speaker to remove slides naming specific tech companies before allowing her talk, and she publicly concluded the summit is "captured" by industry interests and unlikely to invite her back. This directly undercuts Werner's own claim (E7 source) that AI for Good "gives the same platform to people who are highly critical of big tech."
