**Full text (verbatim, David Jordan Soro's words):**
> This week I stumbled on a bookshop in Madrid called Eres lo que lees. You are what you read.
>
> Cheeky name. It stuck with me, though, because if it's true, my current reading pile is telling on me.
>
> Five books open at once, all product, and I didn't plan any pattern. Here's what's on it:
>
> - Money Stories, Rich Mironov. I read his post on designing money stories a few years ago and never stopped using it. The short book is that idea with the examples the posts never had: what the story looks like deal by deal, situation by situation.
>
> - Real Progress, Tim Herbig. Nobody I've read explains defining strategy through OKRs this simply. His frameworks are easy to apply, which is the whole point: they've helped me deliver the message to the teams I coach without translating anything first.
>
> - Artificial Organizations, Barry O'Reilly. The only AI-related book on the pile, and one I'm quite enjoying. Not another "AI changes everything" book: it's honest about how much organisations need to adapt, and concrete about the first steps.
>
> - Incorruptible, Eric Ries. This should be a must-read in business schools. Successful organisations that failed to sustain a healthy business, told directly, so you can see what to avoid before you're the case study.
>
> - The Decision Stack, Martin Eriksson. A new classic. I'm on the first half and it already does the thing most strategy books skip: connecting strategy to the product decisions a team makes every day.
>
> So apparently I am what I read: money, strategy, AI, healthy businesses, and decisions.
>
> Fair enough. That is the job.
>
> What would your pile say about you?
